# Pocket Quiet
A design philosophy for ポケットToDo's App Store presence.

---

## Philosophy

**Pocket Quiet** is the practice of small, careful gestures in vast still air.

It is the recognition that intimacy comes from restraint, not abundance. That a single dark mark on an unbroken field of breath says more than a crowded one. That the value of a small object is amplified by the room it is given to exist.

The discipline derives from Japanese book design — the way Iwanami's small editions cradle each line of type in disciplined margin, the way an Edo-era pillow book treats a passing observation with the gravity of a sutra. It borrows from kakejiku, where one stroke of ink stretches across paper and lets the paper be felt. It borrows from engawa, the verandah between inside and outside — a threshold of contemplation, neither here nor there.

The product itself is a pocket — a place where small unfinished things are kept with care until they can be set down. The visual presence must extend that same gesture. Each artifact must feel like a single page lifted from a small book printed with reverence — meticulously letterpressed, line-broken with attention, signed at the foot with a quiet mark.

## Visual Expression

**Space as the dominant material.** Off-white is not background — it is breath, the medium that everything else floats inside. Generous margins on every edge — three times what convention requires. The screenshot must feel like a postcard set carefully on a wide table, not a fact reported.

**Two typographic voices, never more.** One weighted statement of intent — set at the size of an opening sentence in a book — and one quiet attribution below, half the weight and three-quarters the size. The headline is the chapter heading. The subhead is the gentle aside. Nothing further.

**The screenshot framed, never floated.** A device frame — restrained, deeply considered, dark — establishes the artifact's substance. Beneath it, a soft shadow no harder than the impression of paper resting on paper. The depth must be felt rather than seen. Master-level subtlety: anyone who notices the shadow has missed the point; the eye should simply rest more easily.

**A single mark of provenance.** A tiny numeric index, a horizontal hairline, a small typographic colophon — one quiet detail anchoring the page in a series, suggesting careful editorial intent. The kind of mark you find on the inside of a Japanese hardcover book, italicized and tiny.

**Limited palette, infinite tuning.** Two colors only: sumi (the warm off-white field) and ink (the deep, slightly warm charcoal). Every other tonal value derives from blending these. The restraint is the craft.

## Craft Standards

This must look like the work of a careful press that operates from a small studio and prints two hundred copies. It must look as though it was set by a typesetter who chose every word's position by sight, then chose again. It must look as though the photographer was given two days to make a single image and chose to take none of them. It must look, finally, like the kind of artifact that a careful person would keep on a shelf and return to.

The composition must reward sustained viewing without rewarding speed. A viewer scanning past should feel calm; a viewer who stops should feel that someone at the highest level of their craft thought about this for a long time, and then removed everything that wasn't essential.

Nothing should be added that does not need to be there. Everything that is there should look as though it could not have been placed anywhere else.

## Subtle Conceptual DNA

The hidden reference, present only to those who know: each marketing image is a single leaf from a *makura no sōshi* — Sei Shōnagon's *Pillow Book* — translated into the formal grammar of contemporary minimalist book design. The observation of small things, treated with the reverence usually reserved for great ones.

Those who know will feel it. Those who do not will simply experience a calm, considered artifact.
